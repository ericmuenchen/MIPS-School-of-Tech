Course Description

Intro to Cybersecurity puts you in the seat of a security analyst. Over one semester you'll learn how organizations protect their networks, spot attacks as they happen, and clean up after a breach. The course follows the TestOut CyberDefense Pro curriculum, which pairs plain-language lessons with hands-on labs and simulations, so you're not just reading about security, you're doing it.

The term is built around five real-world skill areas: Security Operations (frameworks, controls, and risk), Threat Intelligence & Hunting (tracking attackers and reading the signs they leave behind), Vulnerability Management (finding and scoring weaknesses before attackers do), Attacks & Defenses (how systems get compromised and how to stop it), and Incident Response & Forensics (containing, investigating, and recovering from an attack). Everything you learn maps directly to the CompTIA CySA+ (CS0-003) certification, so finishing this course leaves you ready to sit a professional industry exam if you ever choose to.

How This Course Works

This course is fully asynchronous: there are no required live sessions, so you can do the work when it fits your schedule. Announcements and feedback come through email and Google Classroom, and the coursework (lessons, labs, simulations, and quizzes) lives in the MICE LMS on the TestOut CyberDefense Pro platform. I'll set weekly checkpoints to help you stay on pace.

Weekly video meetup

Each week I host a 30-minute video meetup where you can share projects, connect with classmates, play games, and discuss emerging technologies. It's recommended for connection and community building, but not required.

What You'll Learn

By the end of the semester you'll think like a defender: able to watch a network, recognize when something is wrong, and take the right action to shut an attack down.

Monitor & detect
Read network traffic and logs to catch threats early using SIEM tools.
Harden & defend
Lock down systems, manage risk, and shrink the attack surface.
Respond & recover
Contain incidents, run digital forensics, and prep for the CySA+ exam.
  • Apply security frameworks and risk-management principles (PCI DSS, ISO 27000) to real scenarios
  • Gather threat intelligence and hunt for Indicators of Compromise across a network
  • Run reconnaissance and vulnerability scans, then score findings with CVSS
  • Recognize and defend against common attacks: SQL injection, phishing, DoS, and malware
  • Use SIEM and SOAR tools to correlate logs and investigate abnormal activity
  • Execute an incident-response plan, maintain chain of custody, and perform digital forensics
  • Administer identity, access controls, and encryption to keep systems compliant
  • Map every skill to CompTIA CySA+ (CS0-003), so the optional certification exam is within reach if you want it

Core Competency Domains

The curriculum is organized around five operational domains, the same areas measured by the CompTIA CySA+ (CS0-003) exam.

Domain 1.0
Monitoring & Log Analysis
Network traffic, port, and socket monitoring; software-process analysis and log management with SIEM tools.
Domain 2.0
Threat Analysis & Detection
Threat and trend analysis; detection with IDS tools, protocol analyzers, and digital-forensic investigation.
Domain 3.0
Risk Analysis & Mitigation
Hardening systems, configuring host firewalls, managing unpatched systems, penetration testing, and honeypots.
Domain 4.0
Incident Response
Resolving malware, ransomware, phishing, and DDoS incidents; analyzing IoCs across endpoints, apps, and networks.
Domain 5.0
Audit & Compliance
Administering user accounts and role-based access control, implementing physical access controls, and system drive encryption.

Grading

Your grade is your total score across assignments, projects, and quizzes. Each week you'll get a progress report showing two numbers: your overall grade, which reflects only the work you've turned in so far, and your relative grade, which also counts anything not yet submitted as a zero. In other words, what your grade would be if the semester ended today.

The Certification Exam Is Optional

This syllabus points toward the CompTIA CySA+ (CS0-003) certification in a few places, so here it is plainly: sitting for that exam is optional. It isn't a course requirement, it isn't part of your grade, and there's nothing you have to register for, pay for, or schedule. You earn full credit for this course by doing the coursework in MICE LMS, and that's the whole requirement. The quizzes and practice exams inside the course do count toward your grade; the certification exam is a separate, outside test, and that one is entirely your call.

If you want to learn more

You can read what the CySA+ covers and how testing works at comptia.org/en-us/certifications/cybersecurity-analyst. The TestOut CyberDefense Pro exam is optional in exactly the same way. If you're wondering whether the certification is worth pursuing, what sitting for it involves, or whether you're ready, email me at muenchen@miprepschool.org and I'll walk you through it. I'm glad to help you go after it, and just as glad if you'd rather not.

Course Policies

Pacing & deadlines

Weekly targets keep your progress steady, but you're welcome to work ahead. If you fall behind, reach out early and we can build a plan to help you catch up before the semester ends.

Because this course is fully asynchronous, there is no penalty for late work. The weekly schedule is a pacing guide to help you stay on track over the semester, not a set of hard deadlines. Staying close to the pace keeps the workload manageable and makes sure you finish every project before the term ends.

Communication

AI & Ethics Policy

The goal is for you to learn to think like a security analyst, and to use every skill you gain responsibly.

AI tools can be great tutors, and security professionals use them every day to speed up analysis. But you are at the beginning of your cybersecurity education. If AI does all the thinking for you, the learning never happens. On the CySA+ exam and on the job, you'll be on your own.

This course also teaches real offensive and defensive techniques. That knowledge comes with responsibility.

  • Use AI to explain a concept, decode an error or log entry, or ask "why did this happen?" — not to generate finished lab answers or quiz responses.
  • You should be able to explain every answer you submit. If work looks copy/pasted, I'll ask to meet with you so you can walk me through your reasoning.
  • Only use the attack and scanning techniques you learn inside the TestOut labs and systems you own or are given permission to test. Using them against anyone else's systems is illegal and grounds for removal from the course.
  • When in doubt, ask me first. I would much rather help you than have you cross a line you can't uncross.
