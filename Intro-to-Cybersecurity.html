Course Description

Intro to Cybersecurity starts with the digital world you already live in and works outward. Over one semester you'll look at what your online life reveals about you, how companies collect and use personal data, how messages are hidden and revealed with codes and ciphers, how the computer in front of you is set up and secured, and how the internet carries a message across the world. It is built for complete beginners: no programming or computer science background is needed.

The term runs through five modules: Cybersecurity and You (digital footprint, personal data, cyber ethics and law, and common attacks), Cryptography (ciphers from the Caesar wheel to public-key encryption, hashing, and digital certificates), Create an Escape Room (a project where you build a cipher-driven escape room of your own), System Administration (operating systems, licensing, user accounts and permissions, and the command line), and IT Concepts (binary, IP addresses, packets, DNS, and the OSI model).

How This Course Works

This course is fully asynchronous: there are no required live sessions, so you can do the work when it fits your schedule. Announcements and feedback come through email and Google Classroom, and the coursework (short videos, interactive lessons, explorations, simulations, free-response prompts, and quizzes) lives in CodeHS. I'll set weekly checkpoints to help you stay on pace.

Weekly video meetup

Each week I host a 30-minute video meetup where you can share projects, connect with classmates, play games, and discuss emerging technologies. It's recommended for connection and community building, but not required.

What You'll Learn

By the end of the semester the digital world will stop being a black box: you'll know what your data is doing, how it is kept secret, and how it gets where it is going.

Own your digital footprint
Know what your online life reveals, who is collecting it, and how to lock it down.
Make and break codes
Encrypt by hand with real ciphers, then see how the same ideas secure the whole web.
Trace the whole system
Set up an operating system, and follow a message from binary to packets to a web page.
  • Manage your digital footprint, your privacy settings, and the data companies collect about you
  • Judge whether a source can be trusted, and where ethics and law draw the line online
  • Spot phishing, malware, and other common attacks, and know what to do about them
  • Encrypt and decrypt messages with real ciphers, and explain how modern encryption protects the web
  • Compare Windows, macOS, and Linux, and set up user accounts, permissions, and the command line
  • Trace a message across the internet: binary, IP addresses, packets, DNS, and the OSI model

Course Modules

Five modules, in the order you'll meet them. You'll see the same five in the schedule below.

Module 1
Cybersecurity and You
Digital footprint and responsibility, personal data and who collects it, judging what you read online, cyber ethics and law, personal data security, the AAA framework and the CIA triad, and common attacks and prevention.
Module 2
Cryptography
Cryptography then, now, and future; symmetric ciphers from Caesar to Rail Fence; asymmetric and public-key encryption; authentication, SSL/TLS certificates, and hashing.
Module 3
Project: Create an Escape Room
Build a cipher-driven escape room of your own: a storyline, at least three cryptographic puzzles, a room layout, a full test run with players, and a finished game.
Module 4
System Administration
Operating systems and updates; comparing Windows, macOS, and Linux; compatibility and file types; software and licensing; application and browser security; user accounts and permissions; and the command line.
Module 5
IT Concepts
Encoding text and images in binary; what the internet is; IP addresses, routing, and packets; how websites work through DNS and HTTP; the OSI model; and the impact of the internet.

Grading

Your grade is your total score across assignments, projects, and quizzes. Each week you'll get a progress report showing two numbers: your overall grade, which reflects only the work you've turned in so far, and your relative grade, which also counts anything not yet submitted as a zero. In other words, what your grade would be if the semester ended today.

Course Policies

Pacing & deadlines

Weekly targets keep your progress steady, but you're welcome to work ahead. If you fall behind, reach out early and we can build a plan to help you catch up before the semester ends.

Late work

Because this course is fully asynchronous, there is no penalty for late work. The weekly schedule is a pacing guide to help you stay on track over the semester, not a set of hard deadlines. Staying close to the pace keeps the workload manageable and makes sure you finish every project before the term ends.

Communication

Email me anytime at muenchen@miprepschool.org or send me a Google chat. I typically respond within one school day.

AI & Ethics Policy

The goal is for you to understand how the digital world really works, and to use what you learn responsibly.

AI is a good tutor and a poor substitute: if it does the thinking, the learning does not happen. And curiosity is the point of this course, because learning how attacks work is how you learn to stop them.

  • Use AI to explain a concept or unstick you on a cipher. Don't use it to write your reflections, your project, or your quiz answers.
  • Be able to explain anything you turn in. If something doesn't sound like you, I'll ask you to walk me through it.
  • Experiment in the CodeHS activities and on your own accounts and devices, not on anyone else's. If there is something you want to try, bring it to me and we'll find a safe way to do it.
  • Ask me anything, including the questions you think you shouldn't ask.
