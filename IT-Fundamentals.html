Course Description

IT Fundamentals introduces you to the ideas behind every corner of modern technology: how computers work, how networks and the internet connect them, and how to keep your data and devices secure. The course runs through CertMaster Learn in eleven modules, each mixing short readings, videos, labs, and interactives so you can see a concept and then try it yourself.

You'll move from Understanding Tech Basics and Data and Privacy through Internet Technologies, Cybersecurity, and Networking, then into Applications and Software, Operating Systems, and Hardware, before finishing with Databases, Coding, and a look at The Future of Tech. Each module builds skills that map directly to the CompTIA Tech+ (FC0-U71) certification exam.

How This Course Works

This course is fully asynchronous: there are no required live sessions, so you can do the work when it fits your schedule. Announcements and feedback come through email and Google Classroom, and the work itself lives in CertMaster Learn. I'll set weekly checkpoints to help you stay on pace.

Weekly video meetup

Each week I host a 30-minute video meetup where you can share projects, connect with classmates, play games, and discuss emerging technologies. It's recommended for connection and community building, but not required.

What You'll Learn

This course pulls back the curtain on the technology you already use every day — what's inside your devices, how they connect, and how to keep them and your data safe.

How tech actually works
The devices, components, and operating systems behind everyday computing.
Keep data and devices secure
Privacy, passwords, encryption, and everyday security best practices.
Think in data and logic
Databases, programming concepts, and structured troubleshooting.
  • Identify computing devices, internal components, peripheral devices, and storage types
  • Use and manage an operating system, including file management and data backup
  • Browse the internet safely and compare internet service and small wireless network options
  • Protect your data with strong passwords, encryption, and security best practices
  • Compare application types and troubleshoot software using the CompTIA methodology
  • Explain database concepts and compare relational database structures
  • Read basic programming logic, data types, and coding principles
  • Explore career paths across the tech industry, from cybersecurity to coding

Grading

Your grade is your total score across assignments, projects, and quizzes. Each week you'll get a progress report showing two numbers: your overall grade, which reflects only the work you've turned in so far, and your relative grade, which also counts anything not yet submitted as a zero — in other words, what your grade would be if the semester ended today.

Course Policies

Pacing & deadlines

Weekly targets keep your progress steady, but you're welcome to work ahead. If you fall behind, reach out early and we can build a plan to help you catch up before the semester ends.

Late work

Because this course is fully asynchronous, there is no penalty for late work. The weekly schedule is a pacing guide to help you stay on track over the semester, not a set of hard deadlines. Staying close to the pace keeps the workload manageable and makes sure you finish every project before the term ends.

Communication

Email me anytime at muenchen@miprepschool.org or send me a Google chat. I typically respond within one school day.

AI Policy

The goal is for you to learn to think like a programmer, not to turn in code you did not write.

AI tools can be great tutors, and professional computer scientists use AI as a standard part of their workflow. But you are just at the beginning of your computer science education. If AI does all the work and thinking for you, learning never happens.

Here is how to acceptably use AI in this course, and anything specific to this class's projects and tools.

  • Use AI to explain a concept, translate an error message, or ask "why doesn't this work?" — not to generate a finished exercise or project.
  • You should be able to explain every line you submit. If I see that you have copy/pasted large sections of code into your projects, I will ask to meet with you so you can demonstrate your understanding.
  • When in doubt, ask me first. I would much rather help you than have you turn in work you cannot explain.
  • Lesson Reviews, Module Quizzes, Checkpoint Reviews, and the Final Exam all run inside CertMaster Learn and must reflect your own understanding — not answers looked up or generated by AI.
