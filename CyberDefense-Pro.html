Course Description

Cyber Defense Pro is a hands-on introduction to cybersecurity operations. Working in a virtual lab environment, you'll scan real networks, chase down vulnerabilities, and investigate the same kinds of attacks security analysts see on the job — then practice containing and reporting on them. By the end of the semester you'll be ready to sit for the CompTIA CySA+ and TestOut CyberDefense Pro certification exams.

The semester moves through nine modules in four arcs: Foundations & Risk Management (regulations, security controls, and patch management), Threat Intelligence & Vulnerability Assessment (threat hunting, scanning, and CVSS scoring), Network & System Security (architecture, IAM, and network attacks like SQL injection and sniffing), and Host-Based Attacks & Security Operations (malware, forensics, SIEM/SOAR, and incident response).

How This Course Works

This course is fully asynchronous: there are no required live sessions, so you can do the work when it fits your schedule. Announcements and feedback come through email and Google Classroom, and the work itself — lessons, virtual labs, and exams — lives in MICE LMS. I'll set weekly checkpoints to help you stay on pace.

Optional weekly meetup

Each week I host an optional 30-minute meeting where you can share projects, connect with classmates, play games, and discuss emerging technologies. It's not required, but it's a fun way to stay connected to the class.

What You'll Learn

You'll work the way real security analysts do: inside virtual labs, against live-fire simulations of the attacks organizations face every day — then practice the reporting and response skills that turn a detection into a resolved incident.

Threat detection & response
Monitor networks, spot intrusions, and act before damage is done.
Real attack simulation
Run the same tools attackers and defenders use, in a safe virtual range.
Certification-ready
Build toward the CompTIA CySA+ and TestOut CyberDefense Pro exams.
  • Apply security regulations, benchmarks (CIS, OWASP), and risk management principles
  • Scan and enumerate networks with tools like Nmap, Wireshark, and Metasploit
  • Score and prioritize vulnerabilities using CVSS and a formal vulnerability management lifecycle
  • Configure firewalls, IDS/IPS, and monitor traffic through a SIEM (Security Onion, Kibana)
  • Identify and exploit web attacks — SQL injection, XSS, and session hijacking — to understand the defense
  • Investigate malware, perform digital forensics, and image and recover data from compromised drives
  • Recognize social engineering, phishing, and denial-of-service attacks in the wild
  • Contain an incident and write it up, from first detection through post-incident reporting

Grading

Your grade is your total score across assignments, projects, and quizzes. Each week you'll get a progress report showing two numbers: your overall grade, which reflects only the work you've turned in so far, and your relative grade, which also counts anything not yet submitted as a zero — in other words, what your grade would be if the semester ended today.

Course Policies

Pacing & deadlines

Weekly targets keep your progress steady, but you're welcome to work ahead. If you fall behind, reach out early and we can build a plan to help you catch up before the semester ends.

Late work

Because this course is fully asynchronous, there is no penalty for late work. The weekly schedule is a pacing guide to help you stay on track over the semester, not a set of hard deadlines. Staying close to the pace keeps the workload manageable and makes sure you finish every project before the term ends.

Communication

Email me anytime at muenchen@miprepschool.org or send me a Google chat. I typically respond within one school day.

AI Policy

The goal is for you to learn to think like a programmer, not to turn in code you did not write.

AI tools can be great tutors, and professional computer scientists use AI as a standard part of their workflow. But you are just at the beginning of your computer science education. If AI does all the work and thinking for you, learning never happens.

Here is how to acceptably use AI in this course, and anything specific to this class's projects and tools.

  • Use AI to explain a concept, translate an error message, or ask "why doesn't this work?" — not to generate a finished exercise or project.
  • You should be able to explain every line you submit. If I see that you have copy/pasted large sections of code into your projects, I will ask to meet with you so you can demonstrate your understanding.
  • When in doubt, ask me first. I would much rather help you than have you turn in work you cannot explain.
  • The virtual labs only build real skills if you drive them yourself — use AI to understand a concept or a command, not to run a lab or write an incident report for you.
