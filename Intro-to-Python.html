Course Description

Introduction to Python Programming introduces you to the fundamentals of computer programming, with an emphasis on building logical thinking and problem-solving skills. You begin by learning to design, code, and test your own programs while applying mathematical concepts, then move on to write more powerful programs of your own. This syllabus covers the first half of the course, Units 1 through 8.

You will start by giving commands to Karel the Dog in a grid world, then learn Basic Python and console interaction, teach your programs to make decisions with conditionals, and write more efficient code using loops. Along the way you will build three real projects: a Mad Libs generator, a quiz game, and a password authenticator. No prior programming experience is needed — the course is highly visual, dynamic, and interactive.

How This Course Works

Each unit is broken into lessons made up of video tutorials, short quizzes, example programs to explore, and hands-on programming exercises. Every content unit ends with a unit test, and project units end with a program you build and submit.

Work at your own pace, with support

Move through the lessons at a pace that works for you. I use CodeHS tools to see where you are and give focused 1-on-1 attention, so if a concept is not clicking, book a 1:1 meeting and we will work through it together.

What You'll Learn

By the end of this semester you will think like a programmer: breaking problems into steps, teaching a program to make decisions, and building real, working software from scratch.

Break problems into steps
Command Karel the Dog and design functions using top-down decomposition.
Make your programs decide
Use conditionals and loops to control what a program does and how many times.
Build three real projects
A Mad Libs generator, a quiz game, and a password authenticator.
  • Define and call functions, and design your own
  • Create variables, work with types, and build arithmetic and string expressions
  • Read user input from the console
  • Comment your code so it stays readable

Grading

Your grade is your total score across assignments, projects, and quizzes. Each week you'll get a progress report showing two numbers: your overall grade, which reflects only the work you've turned in so far, and your relative grade, which also counts anything not yet submitted as a zero — in other words, what your grade would be if the semester ended today.

Course Policies

Pacing & deadlines

The course is self-paced within the term, but staying close to the roadmap keeps you on track for both projects and unit tests. If you fall behind, do not stress — reach out and we will build a plan to catch you up.

Late work

Late work is accepted, and you may revise and resubmit exercises and projects to raise your grade. The goal is that you actually learn to code, so growth counts more than getting it perfect the first time.

Communication

Email me anytime at muenchen@miprepschool.org and I will usually reply within one school day. All lessons, exercises, and feedback live in CodeHS, and you can book a 1:1 meeting whenever you want to work through something together.

AI Policy

The goal is for you to learn to think like a programmer, not to turn in code you did not write.

AI tools can be great tutors, and professional computer scientists use AI as a standard part of their workflow. But you are just at the beginning of your computer science education. If AI does all the work and thinking for you, learning never happens.

Here is how to acceptably use AI in this course, and anything specific to this class's projects and tools.

  • Use AI to explain a concept, translate an error message, or ask "why doesn't this work?" — not to generate a finished exercise or project.
  • You should be able to explain every line you submit. If I see that you have copy/pasted large sections of code into your projects, I will ask to meet with you so you can demonstrate your understanding.
  • When in doubt, ask me first. I would much rather help you than have you turn in work you cannot explain.
