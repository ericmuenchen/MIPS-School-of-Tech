Course Description

In this course you will combine creativity with computer science to build your own interactive games and music. The semester is organized into three hands-on units.

You begin with Animations, using the Game Lab environment to program shapes, sprites, and the draw loop, then respond to keyboard and mouse input with variables and conditionals. In Games you add velocity, collisions, and functions, and work through the game design process to build something playable.

You finish with Music, where you use code to build, remix, and perform original songs. Through sequencing, loops, and functions you'll practice computational thinking, including pattern recognition, algorithmic thinking, and debugging, to design, test, and refine your musical ideas. You'll learn to think like a programmer and create like a musician.

How This Course Works

This is a fully asynchronous online course, so there are no required live meetings. Everything you need lives in Google Classroom, and all coding happens right in your browser. You'll work through lessons at your own pace, with weekly checkpoints to keep your progress on track.

Live support, even in an async class

Each week there's a 30-minute class share-out, where you can talk with classmates about your work and ask me questions live. It's recommended for connection and community building, but not required. I'm also available for 1:1 help sessions.

What You'll Learn

Unit 1 · Animations
  • Use the Game Lab coordinate system to draw with shapes and text
  • Store and reuse information with variables and random numbers
  • Create and control sprites, sprite properties, and the draw loop
  • Respond to keyboard and mouse input using conditionals
  • Build an interactive card that reacts to the person using it
Unit 2 · Games
  • Move sprites with velocity and detect when they collide
  • Handle complex sprite movement and layered collisions
  • Write functions to organize and reuse your code
  • Work through the game design process from idea to playable build
  • Design, test, and refine a game of your own
Unit 3 · Music
  • Sequence and layer tracks to compose original songs
  • Use loops and nested loops to structure music efficiently
  • Write functions to organize and reuse musical sections
  • Recognize patterns and think algorithmically about sound
  • Debug and refine using the problem-solving process
  • Remix a track into a new genre for a final performance

Course Policies

Pacing & deadlines

Weekly targets keep your progress steady, but you're welcome to work ahead. If you fall behind, reach out early and we can build a plan to help you catch up before the semester ends.

Late work

Because this course is fully asynchronous, there is no penalty for late work. The weekly schedule is a pacing guide to help you stay on track over the semester, not a set of hard deadlines. Staying close to the pace keeps the workload manageable and makes sure you finish every project before the term ends. Coding means trying, fixing, and trying again, so you can revise and resubmit any project after you get feedback to improve your grade.

Communication

Email is the best way to reach your instructor; expect a reply within one school day. Google Chat is also a good way to reach me. All lessons, assignments, feedback, and announcements live in Google Classroom, so check in regularly to make sure nothing slips by.

AI Policy

The goal is for you to learn to think like a programmer, not to turn in code you did not write.

AI tools can be great tutors and professional computer scientists use AI as a standard part of their workflow.  But you are just at the beginning of your computer science education. If AI does all the work and thinking for you, learning never happens.

Here is how to acceptably use AI in this course:

  • Use AI to explain a concept, translate an error message, or ask "why doesn't this work?", not to generate a finished exercise or project.
  • You should be able to explain every line you submit. If I see that you have copy/pasted large sections of code into your projects, I will ask to meet with you so you can demonstrate your understanding. 
  • When in doubt, ask me first. I would much rather help you than have you turn in work you cannot explain.
